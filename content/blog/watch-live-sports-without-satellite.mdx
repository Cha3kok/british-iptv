Premium sports coverage — football, motorsport, boxing, cricket, rugby and golf — is spread across several broadcasters in the UK. Following it all through a satellite bundle can cost £40–£60 a month just for the sports add-on, on top of the base package and an 18–24 month contract. The good news is that you no longer need a dish to watch live sport. Here are your options, what they cost and who each one suits.

## Your options at a glance

| Option | Typical cost | Coverage | Contract | Best for |
|--------|-------------|----------|----------|----------|
| Broadcaster day pass | Around £15 per day | One broadcaster's events that day | None | Occasional big events |
| Broadcaster monthly pass | Around £30–£35 a month | One broadcaster's full schedule | None | Fans of one sport or league |
| Single-sport subscription | Around £25–£30 a month | One competition or sport | None | Fans focused on one competition |
| Free-to-air apps | Free | Highlights and selected major tournaments | None | Casual viewers |
| IPTV subscription | From about £5 a month | Sports channels from many broadcasters in one place | None | Regular sports viewers |
| Satellite sports bundle | £65–£120 a month in total | Most live sport | 18–24 months | Homes with slow broadband |

## Option 1: Broadcaster streaming passes

Most major sports broadcasters now sell their channels through a streaming app, either as a **day pass** (around £15) or a **monthly pass** (around £30–£35). There's no contract and you can cancel any time.

**Pros:** official, simple, high quality. **Cons:** each pass only covers that broadcaster's events. Rights to football, motorsport and boxing are split between several companies, so following a full season often means paying for two or three passes at once — which can end up costing as much as satellite.

## Option 2: Single-sport subscriptions

Some competitions and sports — European club football, combat sports and wrestling, for example — sit behind their own subscription service, usually £25–£30 a month. Good value if you follow one competition closely; expensive if you follow several.

## Option 3: Free-to-air sport

Free-to-air broadcasters still show plenty of sport through their apps and channels: highlights programmes, selected live races, and major international football and rugby tournaments. If you mostly watch the big tournaments and catch the rest as highlights, this may be all you need — it costs nothing beyond your TV licence where one is required.

## Option 4: An IPTV subscription

IPTV brings live sports channels together in one subscription and one app, typically for £5–£15 a month with no contract. It works on a Smart TV, Firestick, phone or tablet, and most services include 7-day catch-up so you can rewatch matches you missed.

To get the best experience for live sport:

- Use a **wired connection** — Wi-Fi is the most common cause of buffering on match days.
- Have at least **10 Mbps for HD** and **25 Mbps for 4K**.
- **Test during a busy period** with a free trial, such as a Saturday afternoon or a weekday evening fixture.

## Option 5: Keep satellite (if your broadband is slow)

If your broadband is below about 10 Mbps or drops out often, satellite is still the most reliable way to watch live sport, as it doesn't depend on your internet connection. See our [IPTV vs satellite TV comparison](/blog/iptv-vs-satellite-tv-comparison).

## Which option is right for you?

- **You watch a few big events a year:** free-to-air plus the odd day pass.
- **You follow one league or sport:** that broadcaster's monthly pass or a single-sport subscription.
- **You watch several sports every week:** an IPTV subscription is usually the most affordable way to have everything in one place.
- **Your broadband is slow:** stay with satellite.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I watch live sport without a satellite dish?**
Yes. Streaming passes, single-sport subscriptions, free-to-air apps and IPTV all work over broadband.

**What's the cheapest way to watch live sport in the UK?**
Free-to-air covers major tournaments at no cost. For regular viewing across several sports, an IPTV subscription from about £5 a month is usually the cheapest option.

**What internet speed do I need to stream live sport?**
About 10 Mbps for HD and 25 Mbps for 4K. A wired connection helps most.
