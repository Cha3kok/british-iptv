Premium sports packages carry the biggest football, motorsport, boxing and cricket coverage — but a full satellite sports bundle can cost £40–£60 per month on top of your broadband. Here are the best alternatives for watching every game without the high price tag.

## Option 1: Broadcaster Streaming Passes

Most major sports broadcasters now sell their channels as a standalone streaming pass, either by the day (around £15) or by the month (around £35). There are no contracts and you can cancel anytime. The catch is that each pass only covers that broadcaster's matches, so following a whole season usually means paying for several.

## Option 2: Single-Sport Subscriptions

Some competitions and sports — European football, combat sports, wrestling — sit behind their own subscription services. Monthly costs are typically £25–£30 each. Good value if you only follow one sport, expensive if you follow several.

## Option 3: IPTV with All Sports Channels

A quality IPTV subscription brings live sports channels together in a single package — typically for £7–£10 per month. That covers football, motorsport, boxing, cricket, rugby, golf and much more. Setup works on Firestick, Smart TV, or your phone.

## Option 4: Free-to-Air Channels

Don't overlook free options. Free-to-air broadcasters show highlights programmes, selected live races and major international rugby and football tournaments. These are free with any internet connection via the broadcasters' own apps.

## Verdict

If you watch sport casually, a day pass for the occasional big event is fine. If you're a regular sports viewer, an IPTV subscription puts all your sports channels in one place — in up to 4K — for a fraction of the cost of a satellite bundle.
